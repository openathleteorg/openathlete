Raw figures (time, distance, heart rate) are shown as recorded. Every figure
derived from them has an entry here, and a figure without one is not shown.
Ratios without support, such as the acute:chronic workload ratio, are left out
on purpose.

- **Validated**: backed by the literature.
- **Validated, with limits**: backed by the literature, within the limits given.
- **Estimate**: useful but without solid validation, always labelled in the app.

## Session RPE

**Validated.** Perceived effort (0 to 10) × minutes. It is the load measure best supported across sports, including where heart rate reads poorly.

```text
RPE (0-10) × min
```

**Limits:** It relies on how you rate your sessions: only rated sessions count.

**Sources:** [Foster et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1519/00124278-200102000-00019), [Haddad et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2017.00612)

## TRIMP

**Validated, with limits.** Training load of a session: its duration weighted by heart rate, which counts more and more as effort rises.

```text
min × ΔHR × 0.64 e^(1.92 ΔHR); ♀ 0.86 e^(1.67 ΔHR)
```

**Limits:** It only sees heart rate, so it reflects short efforts and intervals poorly, and depends on your maximum and resting heart rate being right. Comparing it between sports is unreliable.

**Sources:** [Wallace et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-013-2745-1)

## Fitness (CTL)

**Estimate.** Your load of the last weeks, each day fading with a 42-day time constant. It describes what you have absorbed.

```text
CTL(d) = CTL(d-1) + (1 - e^(-1/42)) × (load - CTL(d-1))
```

**Limits:** A description, not a measure of your fitness: it does not predict a performance. It needs about four months of history to settle.

**Sources:** [Clarke & Skiba 2013](https://doi.org/10.1152/advan.00078.2011), [Imbach et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-05392-8)

## Fatigue (ATL)

**Estimate.** Your load of the last days, each day fading with a 7-day time constant. It describes your recent training.

```text
ATL(d) = ATL(d-1) + (1 - e^(-1/7)) × (load - ATL(d-1))
```

**Limits:** A description of recent load, not a measure of how tired you feel.

**Sources:** [Clarke & Skiba 2013](https://doi.org/10.1152/advan.00078.2011), [Imbach et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-05392-8)

## TSB (Training Stress Balance)

**Estimate.** Fitness minus fatigue: negative when recent load is above what you are used to, positive when it is below.

```text
TSB = CTL - ATL
```

**Limits:** The -20 and +25 bands are conventions, not validated limits. Form describes a balance: it diagnoses neither overreaching nor detraining.

**Sources:** [Clarke & Skiba 2013](https://doi.org/10.1152/advan.00078.2011), [Imbach et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-05392-8)

## Intensity

**Validated.** Time spent below, between and above your two thresholds, from your heart rate zones: zones 1-2, 3 and 4-5 on a five-zone setup.

```text
5 zones: Z1-2 | Z3 | Z4-5
```

**Limits:** It describes your training and depends on your zones being right. No distribution is proven better than the others.

**Sources:** [Seiler & Kjerland 2006](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0838.2004.00418.x), [Casado et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijspp.2021-0435)

## Efficiency

**Estimate.** What you produce per heartbeat on steady outings of 40 minutes or more: rising means more output for the same heart rate.

```text
output / HR
```

**Limits:** Heat, hydration and terrain move it too: read the trend, not one outing.

**Sources:** [Rothschild et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-025-05815-0)

## Aerobic decoupling

**Estimate.** How much efficiency drops from the first half of a steady outing to the second.

```text
(EF1 - EF2) / EF1
```

**Limits:** Heat and hydration raise it, and it has no standard definition. The often-quoted 5% limit has no solid basis, so none is shown.

**Sources:** [Rothschild et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-025-05815-0), [Coyle & González-Alonso 2001](https://doi.org/10.1097/00003677-200104000-00009)

## Runs far beyond your usual longest

**Validated, with limits.** Runs over 10% longer than your longest of the 30 days before. In a study of 5,205 runners, such single jumps were linked to more injuries than usual.

```text
distance > 1.1 × longest (30 d)
```

**Limits:** An association across many runners, not your personal risk, and it comes from one observational study.

**Sources:** [Frandsen et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2024-109380)

## Critical speed

**Estimate.** The speed your best efforts of 2 to 20 minutes point to, fitted as distance = reserve + speed × time. It sits near the limit of what you can sustain.

```text
distance = D′ + CS × t (2-20 min)
```

**Limits:** These are bests from training, not maximal tests, so it can read low: on real races the method predicted the marathon within about 8%. Not tested on trails, so not shown there.

**Sources:** [Smyth & Muniz-Pumares 2020](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000002412), [Jones et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0b013e3181d9cf7f)

## Heart rate by output

**Estimate.** Your median heart rate at each speed adjusted for slope, or at each power. Lower at the same output usually means fitter.

```text
median HR by band of grade adjusted speed or power
```

**Limits:** Field tracking linked to progress with moderate correlations (r 0.43 to 0.61). Heat, fatigue and hydration move it too.

**Sources:** [Vesterinen et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000000349)

## How efforts hold

**Estimate.** How much output dropped, or heart rate rose, from the first to the last third of outings of an hour or more.

```text
(last third - first third) / first third, 60 min or more
```

**Limits:** No standard definition exists: this is ours. Heat and dehydration raise it, and no threshold has a solid basis.

**Sources:** [Smyth et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-022-01680-5), [Coyle & González-Alonso 2001](https://doi.org/10.1097/00003677-200104000-00009)

## Do your records agree?

**Estimate.** Each record against the pace another one implies, with the usual distance-time exponents. A dot above the band: that distance has room to improve.

```text
T2 = T1 × (D2 / D1)^k, k 1.04-1.08 (to 1.15 for the marathon)
```

**Limits:** Exponents vary across runners, and bests from training are not always maximal. A check, not a prediction.

**Sources:** [Vickers & Vertosick 2016](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13102-016-0052-y)

## Likely race times

**Estimate.** A range drawn from one best, with the usual distance-time exponents. The marathon range is wider, as most runners fade more than the formula.

```text
T2 = T1 × (D2 / D1)^k, k 1.04-1.08; marathon k 1.07-1.15
```

**Limits:** It assumes training for that distance and flat roads. It does not predict trail or ultra races, and a training best can stay below your level.

**Sources:** [Vickers & Vertosick 2016](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13102-016-0052-y)

## HRV (7-day ln rMSSD)

**Validated, with limits.** The 7-day rolling mean of the logarithm of your overnight variability. Read it against itself: a lasting change matters more than one night.

```text
mean(ln rMSSD, 7 days)
```

**Limits:** Watch readings need good nights of data, sources are not interchangeable, and it is not a readiness score.

**Sources:** [Plews et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-013-0071-8), [Buchheit 2014](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2014.00073)

## Critical swim speed

**Validated, with limits.** The pace given by the line between your 200 m and 400 m times. It is linked to swimming performance.

```text
CSS = (400 - 200) / (T400 - T200)
```

**Limits:** A test pace, not a physiological threshold: it differs from the maximal lactate steady state. It only holds for times swum flat out.

**Sources:** [Wakayoshi et al. 1992](https://doi.org/10.1007/bf00717953), [Dekerle et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-2004-821227)

## Critical power

**Validated, with limits.** The power your best efforts of 2 to 20 minutes point to, fitted as work = reserve + power × time. It sits near the limit of what you can sustain.

```text
work = W′ + CP × t (2-20 min)
```

**Limits:** These are bests from training, not maximal tests: compared with a lab protocol, individual gaps can reach tens of watts. Only hard efforts make it reliable.

**Sources:** [Jones et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0b013e3181d9cf7f), [Karsten et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-014-3001-z)

## Monotony (7 days)

**Estimate.** How alike your days of load were this week: the mean over the spread. Even days give a high figure, hard and easy days a low one.

```text
mean(daily load, 7 d) / SD(daily load, 7 d)
```

**Limits:** From a small study of 25 athletes, and the often-quoted limit of 2 is unverified: read it as a description.

**Sources:** [Foster 1998](https://doi.org/10.1097/00005768-199807000-00023)

## GAP

**Validated, with limits.** The flat pace that costs the same energy as your pace on this slope, from energy costs measured on runners.

```text
v × Cr(i) / Cr(0); Cr = 155.4i⁵ - 30.4i⁴ - 43.3i³ + 46.3i² + 19.5i + 3.6
```

**Limits:** Measured on a treadmill: it knows nothing of technical terrain, and is less reliable on trails.

**Sources:** [Minetti et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1152/japplphysiol.01177.2001)
